The Dingle is a local nature reserve run by Anglesey County Council. It follows the Afon Cefni up a wooded valley from St Cyngar's Church in Llangefni towards Cefni Reservoir. Entry is free.

### The walk

- 1.5-mile boardwalk, suitable for wheelchairs and pushchairs
- Mostly flat. The decking can be slippery after heavy rain
- Extend to a 6-mile loop around Cefni Reservoir
- Weekly Parkrun

### Red squirrels

- Often seen on the boardwalk handrails
- Look out for the feeding stations higher up the valley
- Red squirrels don't hibernate, so you can see them all year

### Other wildlife

- Kingfishers, dippers, herons, grey wagtails and woodpeckers
- Tawny owls, often heard at night, and eight of Britain's sixteen bat species at dusk
- Bluebells under the oaks in spring, and wild daffodils in Coed Plas behind the church

### Sculptures

- A giant dragonfly and giant seed pods
- Split oak timbers carved with the poem *Nant y Pandy* by local poet Rolant o Fôn

### Parking

- Dingle pay & display car park (free with a blue badge)
